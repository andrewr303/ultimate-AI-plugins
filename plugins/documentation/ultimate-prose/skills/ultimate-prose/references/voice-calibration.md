# Voice calibration and meaning-first editing

Use this when a draft feels generic, the user asks for closer voice matching, or a substantial piece needs calibration. The goal is a useful message in the requested voice, with its meaning intact. It is not a fingerprint, an authorship test, or a target score.

## Choose evidence for this task

When relevant examples are already available, prefer writing with a comparable audience, channel, purpose, and degree of formality. A short vendor question is a better guide for another vendor question than a political comment, founder biography, or investor pitch. Recency helps only when the sample is relevant. Do not fetch a private corpus or a whole account without task authorization.

Keep the current draft, facts, voice examples, and desired output separate. Learn a pattern of reasoning or relationship management; do not borrow an example's names, claims, experiences, or private details. If no comparable sample is available, use the existing prose profile and the task. Do not interrupt a routine edit to demand more examples.

A compiled profile may contain several different evidence types:

- An attributed source quotation is reported evidence of wording, with its source and channel limits.
- A sent or posted item shows delivered wording; assistance may be unknown.
- A prepared bio or example is an editorial product unless authorship is established.
- A summary of earlier skill rules is an inherited preference report, not another writing sample.
- Several summaries repeating one statistic or quotation have one evidence lineage. Repetition does not create independent corroboration.

Do not treat the new attachment's references to older profiles as a new corpus or recomputed measurements. Do not train the voice on assistant-written bios, on this reference's synthetic examples, or on another writer's persona.

## Andrew's useful reasoning cues

These are flexible editorial defaults, not a required sequence or a quota. All examples below are synthetic and do not describe Andrew's actual projects or experiences.

| Cue | What to preserve | What not to add |
|---|---|---|
| Direct operational point | The answer, decision, or useful question near the start | A ceremonial hook or a company introduction to a narrow reply |
| Answerable diagnostic question | Supplied alternatives and room for another explanation | An asserted cause, invented research, or an artificially exhaustive menu |
| Goal and minimum outcome | The distinction between the ideal and an acceptable fallback | A stronger promise disguised as smoother wording |
| Concrete mechanism and constraint | The condition that actually connects action to result | An invented "because" or a technical inventory irrelevant to the reader |
| Grounded warmth | Appreciation for the actual response, help, or review | Exaggerated gratitude, a forced personal anecdote, or clinical interpretation |
| Candid ownership | Who did the work and what remains rough, untested, or uncertain | A team, polished launch, confident forecast, or completed result not supplied |

**Diagnostic question:** "Is the constraint budget, timing, fit, or something else?" The options make the question easier to answer; they do not establish a diagnosis. Keep the supplied alternatives and open end.

**Meaningful refinement:** "The aim is the full workflow; if validation needs longer, the export is the minimum useful result." Use this relationship only when the source supplies it. A thought such as "or at least" can narrow the claim; do not remove that distinction as mere hesitation, and do not manufacture hesitation to sound natural.

**Recipient-relevant proof:** "Retries no longer create a second order in the pilot." If those are the supplied facts, they are more useful to a buyer than an inventory of workers and queues. A technical reader may need that inventory; choose by purpose rather than banning technical detail.

## Review what each sentence contributes

Read for the actual proposition before looking for stock words. A sentence can answer, explain a mechanism, provide evidence, set a condition, distinguish objects, ask a question, or manage a relationship. Several sentences can serve one purpose, but a later sentence should earn its space.

For suspected filler, ask what new information disappears if it is cut. If it only announces significance, restates a promise with new adjectives, or sets up a reveal, remove it or replace it with a supplied fact. Do not add a metric, benefit, source, or causal link just to fill the gap.

For a narrow question, give the answer and only the explanation needed to use it. Stop when the question is answered; do not append a general rule that restates the same conclusion. When clauses share a scope or status, state that qualification once where it clearly governs them. Repeat it only when omitting it would create ambiguity; compression must not imply a broader result.

For repetition, compare the claims and their scope rather than word overlap. A repeated idea may add a real exception, minimum outcome, consequence, or separate operating instruction. Keep that addition. A necessary noun can recur; a necessary instruction can appear in separate sections. Never delete every repeated word or force synonym variation.

For referent drift, identify whether the terms name the same thing. A model, agent, and tool can be distinct actors. Do not collapse them into one label to avoid a supposed tell, or rotate accurate terms merely for variety.

For an empty closing, check whether it adds an action or implication the recipient needs. Delete a duplicate recap or motivational flourish without replacing it with a cleverer slogan. A useful courteous close or simple coordination sentence can stay.

## When voice and pattern cleanup conflict

Voice the draft already had outranks a pattern cleanup: a stated opinion, mixed feelings, a first-person choice Andrew could explain, a real aside. Count a habit only when it appears at least twice, record a rule with its context, and note what the samples do not cover. Leave out a sample known to be assistant-drafted, even if Andrew sent it. Borrow habits, never passages. Ask of the result: does this sound like Andrew, or like someone describing Andrew?

## Preserve honest texture

Good flow does not require making every sentence equally complete, smooth, or emphatic. Preserve a useful concession, uncertainty, self-refinement, ordinary short answer, or lumpy paragraph when it carries meaning. Correct actual errors without adding random fragments, slang, typos, fake vulnerability, forced humor, or deliberately uneven punctuation to imitate human writing.

A real contrast can correct scope. A three-item list can have three necessary items. An opening with "I" can fit supplied personal or build-note content. A literal technical term can be the accurate term. The current request and the actual passage decide; an old profile's blanket ban is not an automatic rewrite command.

If the user requests story takeaways, derive each from supplied events and preserve the requested format when supported. Do not invent a scene, dialogue, emotion, customer effect, or extra lesson to fill a numbered structure. If the material cannot support the requested number, flag the narrow gap or give the supported number rather than padding. A story does not need a lesson list unless the task calls for one.

## Mechanical suggestions have a smaller job

The scanners inspect surface patterns. The new `prose.redundant-acronym` family points out a tiny set of uppercase acronym phrases, such as PIN number and ATM machines. It is a local redundancy suggestion, not an AI sign or grammatical verdict. Quoted text, code, and technical addresses follow the existing scope rules. Definitions, lowercase homographs, and compound modifiers must remain intact.

The scanner neither determines semantic identity nor decides that a sentence adds no information. Do that review in context. Do not replace a warned word with a synonym just to obtain a clean scan. Never optimize a lexical density, sentence-length band, or tell count as a proxy for the reader's result.

## Check meaning and voice separately

First check the facts, uncertainty, attribution, agency, relationships, quantities, and commitments. A voice match cannot compensate for changing them. Then check whether the message uses relevant reasoning, warmth, and register without importing biography. Finally inspect the mechanical suggestions and output shape.

In a comparison, keep calibration examples separate from the outputs being judged. Use identical tasks, include keep-as-written cases, anonymize the outputs, and allow ties. Treat a small model-agent review as a smoke test, not proof of Andrew's preference or a measured reduction in AI-sounding prose.
