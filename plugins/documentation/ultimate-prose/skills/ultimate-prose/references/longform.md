# Authored long-form

Use for founder posts, essays, investor narratives and letters, talk or pitch scripts, and long LinkedIn or X pieces. It adds positive craft after the subtraction pass and never overrides factual fidelity, claim state, Andrew's voice defaults, the channel, or the brief.

## Decide what the reader carries away

Picture one reader and separate what they already know from what they still need; that gap sets the scope. Choose one takeaway suited to the register:

- **Founder post:** a real operating observation, the evidence or example behind it, and the consequence. A hook is optional; a lesson is owed only if the events support one.
- **Investor narrative or update:** what changed, what the evidence supports, the remaining risk, and the next milestone, with shipped, in-progress, and planned work kept separate.
- **Essay or analysis:** a position someone could dispute, or a mechanism the reader can follow, with the strongest real limitation stated.
- **Talk or pitch script:** one thought a listener can keep without rereading, with signposts a listener can follow once and repetition that helps an audience.
- **Story:** events from supplied material and a reason to keep reading; no invented scene or padded lesson.

## Make each sentence pay

Read each sentence against the one before it. It should add a fact, mechanism, example, qualification, consequence, or a useful change in pressure. Cut throat-clearing, duplicated explanation, and any conclusion that exists only because the form seems to expect one.

Andrew's prose joins a claim to its cause, condition, or qualification, so keep connected reasoning together and use longer sentences when the reasoning needs them. Break a sentence only when it holds unrelated ideas at once, buries the decision, or loses its subject. Splitting a causal chain into short sentences to look punchy is a defect in his voice, not a fix.

## Give the opening one job

The first sentence should make the intended reader want the second while starting the actual work: a supported observation, number, decision, scene, or claim. Avoid a definition, a market panorama, an announcement of intent, or a rhetorical-question hook. Never manufacture a startling number or scene; if the source has no honest opening material, start with the plain point or mark a TK.

## Make the piece develop

Each paragraph should answer something the previous one raised, complicate it, show it, or make the next one necessary. If paragraphs can trade places without loss, the piece is a list dressed as paragraphs. Name the relation at a join that carries the argument (because, although, once, if, so, which), but only when the source supports it; an invented "because" is a factual change.

For an argument, state Andrew's leaning and give the strongest real objection a paragraph near the end, then answer or concede it. Never invent a weak opponent. Updates and confirmations do not owe a debate.

## The author test

For each paragraph, ask whether it could appear nearly word for word in another founder's post on the same subject. If it could, it is filler however well made. What survives is his own material: what he observed, measured, built, decided, or changed his mind about. A draft without it has a sourcing problem, so mark the gap with `[TK: question]` or ask one focused question (see `references/interview.md`) instead of disguising the gap with polish.

## Put craft back

Subtraction alone leaves colder prose. After cutting, inspect the passage that needs the most weight:

- **Evidence:** is the strongest supported example, number, or artifact next to the claim it supports?
- **Stance:** what does Andrew want, prefer, doubt, or refuse here? Analytical writing still states the recommendation.
- **Rhythm:** let syntax carry the relation between thoughts, never missing reasoning.
- **Warmth:** restore grounded appreciation, humor, or candor that the draft had and the edit erased.
- **Closing line:** keep one the preceding material earned, and cut a kicker that performs a depth the piece did not build.

When craft needs material the source lacks, ask or leave a TK rather than inventing an anecdote, reaction, or result.

## Rewrite, listen, stop

Real rewriting may move the strongest paragraph to the top, merge two sections, or cut the line the draft was proudest of, within the scope Andrew authorized. Smoothing every rough edge is not rewriting when the rough edge carries his thought.

Read the finished piece once and fix what obstructs the reader: plodding paragraphs, breathless clauses, repeated sentence shapes, and words he would never say on a call. Then fix the single weakest remaining material issue and stop, because repeated polishing passes converge on a flat, uniform register.
