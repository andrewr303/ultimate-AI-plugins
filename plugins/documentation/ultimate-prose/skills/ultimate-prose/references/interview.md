# Substance gaps and the co-write interview

An authored piece (founder post, essay, investor narrative, talk, LinkedIn story) is only as good as the material behind it. When the brief lacks Andrew's own observations, numbers, or judgment, polish cannot supply them, and inventing them breaks his standing rule against fabricated experience, traction, customers, metrics, partnerships, or investor interest.

## Default: draft what is supported and mark the rest

Andrew's standing preference is to infer and proceed, with at most one focused clarification, rather than an intake interview. So without an explicit request for an interview:

- When most of the piece is supported, draft it and mark each gap inline as `[TK: specific question]`, for example `[TK: how many creator calls before you noticed this? A rough number is fine.]`. List the open TK questions after the draft.
- When the missing material is the piece itself (a post about "lessons from my first 50 calls" with no lessons supplied), ask one focused question for the raw material instead of drafting: "Give me the two or three things you actually heard, in your own words; rough is fine."
- Never fill a gap with a plausible-sounding anecdote, quote, count, or result. A plain true sentence is better than a vivid invented one.

## Opt-in interview

Run the full interview only when Andrew asks for it (`MODE: interview`, "interview me", "co-write this with me"). Then ask for one untidied answer and wait for it before drafting:

> Talk this through in one take, out loud or typed, without organizing it first. Tell me what happened, why you want to write it now, who you want to reach, what you believe, and the examples or doubts that make it yours. Use real names and numbers only if you are comfortable publishing them, and skip private details instead of blurring them.

Offer a few of these prompts as aids, not as a questionnaire:

- What happened this week that made you want to write this: a call, a result, a bug, a pushback?
- Picture one reader (an investor, a customer, a peer founder, a hiring manager). What do they already know, and what should change for them?
- Who disagrees with you, and what is their strongest real argument?
- What do you say about this on calls that you have never written down?
- Which example from your own work carries the point? What happened, and what changed?
- What would you concede under questioning, or what have you changed your mind about?
- What would you cut from the version every other founder would write?

Also ask for his existing posts, notes, or decks on the topic, and treat them as source text with their dates and attribution intact.

## When a draft already exists

Ask at most three questions, aimed at the hollow parts:

```text
This paragraph says [claim]. What is your evidence, and how sure are you?
The example could belong to any founder. What is your version of it?
The ending restates the point. What should the reader do or reconsider?
```

## Turn the answer into prose

1. Extract the point, usable phrases, examples, uncertainties, and the order in which he worked the idea out.
2. Build the spine from his language. Cut true repetition and remarks addressed to the interviewer, and reorder only when that clarifies the development.
3. Edit toward his business register (complete, connected sentences, no dashes, straight quotes) while protecting coined phrases, trade terms, real asides, and mixed feelings. If an edit would change the thought, keep his wording or show both.
4. Add model-written material only for a stated job: sourced research, a definition, a factual bridge, or compression. Keep researched facts separate from his experience and attributed to their sources.
5. Leave `[TK: precise question]` wherever material is still missing.
6. Add a short provenance note after the draft, outside the ready-to-send text, only when the draft mixes his material with model-supplied research or has open TK items:

```text
Author material:     which sections came from Andrew's answer or prior writing
Model contribution:  research, structure, connective prose, or none
Open items:          the [TK] questions, or none
```

For a named file, put the note in the reply rather than the file. The note describes the actual process; it is not an authorship or detector claim.
