# Tone controls

Tone describes how a piece meets its reader. It does not supply facts, credentials, personal history, or a default identity. Choose from the user's language; the labels below are starting points, not a closed menu.

Keep tone separate from formality, certainty, length, and detail. Use intensity sparingly: stronger playful style adds more comic timing, not random errors; stronger analytical style adds more explicit reasoning, not unsupported precision. Unless requested, do not explain your tone settings in the output.

## Six core choices

| Tone | Observable choices | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Plain / direct | Familiar words, accurate verbs, a clear point and next action where relevant; explain only what the reader needs | Brusqueness, chopped reasoning, removing useful courtesy or detail |
| Warm | Acknowledge a real contribution or circumstance; address the person naturally; keep help or care concrete | Invented familiarity, lavish thanks, fake shared memories, automatic reassurance |
| Analytical | State the question, show relevant evidence and relationships, distinguish alternatives and uncertainty | Formulaic pros/cons, unnecessary caveats, invented mechanisms, metric theater |
| Bold | Clear positions, strong supported verbs, decisive framing, an unambiguous request or boundary | Inflated certainty, aggression, false urgency, superlatives without evidence |
| Playful | Light surprise, apt imagery, timing, or a small joke that fits the relationship and stakes | Forced puns, emoji clutter, infantilizing the reader, joking about harm or a serious disclosure |
| Expert | Precise concepts, correct terminology, relevant distinctions, the detail this audience needs; translate jargon where needed | Credential performance, needless jargon, excessive length, condescension, claiming expertise on the user's behalf |

## Same facts, six expressions

These are synthetic demonstrations, not templates or actual events. Every version uses the same facts: **the draft is ready; feedback is requested by Thursday; publication is planned for Monday, subject to that feedback.** No version invents results or strengthens the plan into a guarantee.

**Plain / direct:** The draft is ready. Please send feedback by Thursday. Publication is planned for Monday, subject to that feedback.

**Warm:** The draft is ready for your review. I'd appreciate your feedback by Thursday. We're planning to publish on Monday, subject to that feedback.

**Analytical:** The draft is ready. Monday's planned publication depends on the feedback; please send it by Thursday.

**Bold:** The draft is ready. Send feedback by Thursday. Monday is the planned publication date, subject to that feedback.

**Playful:** The draft is ready for your red pen. Feedback by Thursday, please; publication is planned for Monday, subject to that feedback.

**Expert:** The draft is ready for review. Please return feedback by Thursday; Monday remains the planned publication date, subject to that review.

The differences can be modest for a simple task. Do not invent a technical process to make the expert version distinct or add an anecdote to distinguish warm from plain. On a complex brief, expertise is more visible in the quality of distinctions and explanation. This shared example's phrasing is illustrative; do not reuse it mechanically.

## Additional presets

| Tone | Useful choices | Keep in view |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational | Everyday syntax, natural contractions, direct address where appropriate | Informality does not require slang, typos, or constant questions |
| Formal | Complete phrasing, controlled idiom, conventional address and structure when the setting needs them | Avoid archaic bureaucracy and nominalized verbs without purpose |
| Diplomatic | State shared context, make the disagreement or boundary precise, propose a workable step if supplied | Do not bury a refusal or imply approval |
| Empathetic | Acknowledge the actual difficulty, respond to the stated need, give space where the situation calls for it | Do not diagnose, claim to know exactly how someone feels, or promise a good outcome |
| Persuasive | Connect a supported benefit to this reader's problem and offer a proportionate action | No fake social proof, scarcity, urgency, testimonials, or superiority |
| Reflective | Explore a real observation, ambiguity, or change of mind with room for thought | Do not manufacture a life lesson or personal transformation |
| Lyrical | Use imagery, sound, pacing, and selected detail suited to the subject | Avoid stacked metaphors and ornamental abstraction; factual scenes still need sources |
| Skeptical | Examine assumptions, test scope, identify alternatives, ask answerable questions | Avoid cynicism as a substitute for evidence |
| Witty / dry | Understatement, precise incongruity, economical timing | Do not force a joke into every paragraph or turn a person into the target |
| Understated | Restrained adjectives, specific observation, room for the reader's inference | Do not remove a material warning or needed emphasis |
| Celebratory | Name the actual achievement and acknowledge real contributions | Avoid imaginary milestones, superlatives, or manufactured emotion |
| Urgent | Put the verified time-sensitive action and consequence early | Never invent a deadline or emergency; preserve conditional timing |

Other requests can work: tender, ominous, irreverent, academic, journalistic, romantic, solemn, technical, or curious. Translate the request into choices that fit the piece. Do not apply a business-writing template to a poem, speech, story, or intimate message. If the intended meaning of a niche tone is unclear but the task is still workable, use a modest interpretation; ask only if different readings would materially change the result.

## Blends and boundaries

- **Warm + firm:** acknowledge the person's situation and state the boundary clearly. "Thanks for explaining the delay. I still need the signed copy before we can proceed." Use only when that dependency is supplied.
- **Expert + conversational:** keep accurate terms and explain what they mean in ordinary language; omit the credential display.
- **Analytical + bold:** state the recommendation, show the evidence that supports it, and preserve the caveat that could change it.
- **Playful + professional:** use a small, relevant turn of phrase while keeping names, dates, requests, and boundaries clear.
- **Formal + warm:** respectful address and complete phrasing can coexist with a specific thank-you or acknowledgment.
- **Persuasive + understated:** explain the real fit and make the action easy to assess; do not create a dramatic reveal.

With several requested tones, use a primary tone and distribute the modifiers where they help. A warm opening and a precise analytical body can be more natural than forcing every sentence to perform both. An explicit "no jokes" or "no exclamation marks" is a concrete constraint and takes precedence over a general playful or celebratory label.

## Facts across tone changes

Check these independently of style:

- "Might launch in June" stays conditional, even in a bold version.
- "I can't take this on" stays a refusal, even in a warm version.
- "We saw a change in the sample" does not become a universal result in an expert version.
- A personal note gains no invented memory, feeling, or relationship in an empathetic version.
- A playful metaphor can be fictional imagery; a factual story does not gain fabricated dialogue or events.
- A persuasive request keeps the user's actual price, deadline, promise, and evidence.

## Language and culture

Use the requested language and appropriate idiom rather than translating English stock phrasing word for word. Preserve the user's dialect, honorifics, spelling, and accessibility choices when they are intentional. Do not infer these from identity labels. If locale materially changes address, legal wording, or date interpretation and cannot be inferred, ask or preserve the supplied form. Tone is not permission to caricature a culture or social group.
