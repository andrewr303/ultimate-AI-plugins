# Make content safeguards operational for UGC and generated content

## Map every contribution surface

Identify where users can create, generate, upload, share or expose content to other people. Include text, images, video, audio, profiles, chat, creator experiences and content generated through an AI service. The reviewed Guideline 1.2 explicitly includes AI content creation; a hosted model or web-based content service does not remove the app's moderation, rights or age responsibilities.

Describe the actual audience, discoverability, anonymous/random matching, contact features and routes for reporting abuse. Review the complete interaction, not just the posting screen. Content hidden in a notification, widget, public profile or shared link can have a different exposure and age context from the author's editor.

## Prepare inputs and operational ownership

Collect the community/product rules, filtering approach, report categories, blocking semantics, support contact and staff/service responsible for responses. Record how reports enter that operation and how harmful content can be removed or restricted. A UI button with no functioning destination is not a moderation system. Do not fabricate a response-time guarantee; show the actual process and current owner.

Separate content moderation from private-data handling and intellectual-property claims. A piece of content being allowed by the app's community rules does not establish a right to distribute it. Sending user inputs or personal data to a third-party AI service also needs the applicable privacy disclosure/permission evidence; the fact that a model is useful is not consent.

## Workflow

1. Enumerate creation, discovery, messaging and resharing routes using safe synthetic examples.
2. Match the current required safeguards to each route: filtering before posting where applicable, reports and timely handling, blocking abusive users and published contact information.
3. Exercise what happens after a report, a block and a removal. Verify effects on notifications, shared links, search and other relevant surfaces.
4. Evaluate whether the product is primarily an excluded or harmful experience; adding a report button is not a universal cure for the app's core function.
5. Apply the actual age restrictions to creator content that can exceed the app's age rating. Do not infer age or consent from a profile label alone.
6. Prepare accurate review instructions explaining how the safeguards can be tested and which external services support them.

## Failure and abuse cases

Use harmless fixtures that represent a blocked sender, a report from a second account, an appealed removal, content available through an old link and an anonymous chat interaction. Avoid distributing real abusive media or private reports in test evidence. Verify that blocking is not merely cosmetic while the same sender can continue through another product surface.

Check a generated-content flow that bypasses the ordinary upload filter, a report queue with no active receiver, and a backend removal that leaves cached content public. Where the implementation cannot establish the current safeguard or moderation capacity, mark the exact fact unknown rather than creating a reassuring policy statement.

## Evidence and result

Record the tested build, content surface, actor roles, report/block/removal transitions and operational ownership. A demonstrated missing safeguard or unreachable report/support path is a blocker. Unverified third-party moderation behavior is unknown. Not applicable needs an evidence-backed absence of the content/contribution surface, not a claim that generated content is not UGC. Satisfied evidence covers the reviewed routes and cases only.

Complete the requested implementation or review packet within scope. Do not send reports, contact users, publish policy changes or transmit private content to an external service unless that action is actually authorized. Keep App Review acceptance and legal rights judgments separate from the local safeguard tests.
