# Separate age ratings, Kids Category and parental-consent facts

## Use the actual audience and capability inventory

List the app's content, frequency, social/chat features, web access, advertising, purchases, user-generated material and controls. The age questionnaire should describe this real product rather than a desired marketing rating. Ratings can vary by region and OS generation; preserve the values and explanations returned for the selected distribution instead of applying one hardcoded age table worldwide.

Kids Category participation is a distinct decision with continuing expectations. A visually playful app is not automatically in that category, and deselecting the category is not a shortcut around prior commitments. Read current category, platform and metadata conditions, including limits on child-directed wording, before changing the listing.

## Distinguish three separate questions

First, what content/capabilities determine the store age rating? Second, what Kids Category rules apply to the app and its history? Third, what actual parental consent or age-assurance obligations apply to this product and jurisdiction? A parental gate for a purchase or external link is not automatically valid consent to collect a child's personal data.

Use the current Apple primary pages and owner-confirmed legal/product facts. Do not manufacture a date of birth, infer a user's age from appearance or select an age-assurance method without a real requirement. Where an available platform signal provides a range or a consent state, preserve that meaning and its unavailable/unknown states instead of converting it into a precise age.

## Workflow

1. Compile content and capability evidence from the actual release, including third-party content and SDKs.
2. Complete or prepare the current questionnaire truthfully. Use a supported higher-rating override when the owner has a real applicable requirement, and retain region-specific documentation where necessary.
3. Inspect Kids Category selection history and the corresponding ongoing restrictions on links, purchasing opportunities, analytics and ads.
4. Evaluate the actual SDK data flow against the specific current guideline, not a marketing shorthand. The reviewed child-safety page and Guideline1.3 have wording differences about third-party data; retain that conflict rather than treating consent as an automatic loophole.
5. Test the applicable gate and age/consent handling without collecting unnecessary child information.
6. Reconcile the app's controls, listing, privacy policy and regional commerce behavior before a dependent submission.

## Regional and commercial boundaries

The reviewed EU commerce page contains age-dependent protections for alternative payment routes and recently effective terms. Those conditions depend on the storefront, agreement, app category and applicable age/consent facts. Do not extend them to every country or reuse an old fee/age chart as proof. An unavailable age signal is not a grant of permission.

If legal qualification is missing, identify the exact unresolved claim and owner while finishing independent technical work. Do not silently accept an agreement or mark a parental-consent checkbox for the publisher. A preparation task can produce a reviewable matrix without changing the remote classification.

## Verification cases and evidence

Cover a child/teen/unknown signal where supported, a denied parent request, a restored session, blocked external links/purchases and content that exceeds the selected age range. Test whether third-party ads are actually age-appropriate and whether the relevant operational review/policies exist; a category label alone proves none of that.

Use blocker for a demonstrated incompatible control, rating answer or prohibited flow; unknown for unconfirmed legal/SDK behavior; not applicable with a specific audience/channel reason; and satisfied for the actual evaluated scope. Keep the artifact version, source section/date and owner evidence in the packet. Neither a questionnaire saved successfully nor a passing test proves statutory child-privacy compliance or App Store approval.
