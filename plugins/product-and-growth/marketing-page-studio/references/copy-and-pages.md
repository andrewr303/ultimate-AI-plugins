# Conversion copy and multi-page product stories

## From feature to supported benefit

Write a capability, the action a user takes, the resulting product state and the bounded benefit. A support-inbox example can say "Group the sample requests by intent" when the fixture does that. It cannot claim "Resolve every customer issue instantly" from a local grouping animation.

Use exact product terminology and concrete verbs. Headlines should say what changes for the user. Supporting copy explains how and names relevant constraints. Keep an internal map from each factual statement to an evidence claim ID. Supply qualifications near the claim rather than hiding them in unrelated footer copy.

## Promise and CTA

Make the product and audience recognizable in the initial viewport. The primary CTA should name a real next action such as "Explore the demo", "Start a trial" or "Book a walkthrough". Match its destination and requirements. Do not write "Start free" if pricing or eligibility is unknown.

Use secondary links for a reader who needs more proof. Repeating the primary action at appropriate points is useful; identical CTA bands after every paragraph are usually noise.

## Page types

| Page | Specific job | Required product material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Orient and motivate the next step | Clear promise, representative workflow, proof, CTA |
| Feature | Explain one capability deeply | Input/action/result, detail view, constraints, relevant next step |
| Workflow | Make a sequence understandable | Real steps in order, transitions, recovery, resulting artifact |
| Use case | Show fit for a context | Labeled scenario, relevant inputs and bounded outcome |
| Comparison | Help a grounded choice | Current comparable facts, sources, dates and fair scope |
| Pricing | Explain a verified offer | Prices, billing units, inclusions, restrictions and valid destination |

A feature page needs its own question and example; it should not repeat the homepage hero and change the title. Keep navigation names, current-route indication, typography and CTA meaning consistent. Use breadcrumbs only when the hierarchy benefits from them.

## Screenshot-only projects

Describe what the supplied state shows. Do not invent absent menus, workflow logic or success behavior as if verified. An illustrative interactive prototype can be useful if labeled and isolated; its generated UI must not be presented as a capture. Record missing proof and choose copy that remains accurate.

## Readability and voice

Write for scanning and then understanding: specific headings, short supporting paragraphs, clear labels. Avoid unexplained internal terms. Use the existing brand voice where supplied. Keep legal or product limitations readable without overwhelming the main path.

Check the browser after copy edits. Headline wrapping, button length, translated strings and narrow-width overflow can change hierarchy. A polished text document is not evidence that the same copy fits the page.

## Claim review

Review customers, integrations, logos, security, pricing, numerical results and comparative superlatives with particular care. Do not borrow upstream demo evidence. If proof is absent, omit the statement, attribute a supplied claim or rewrite around a demonstrable action. Mark planned features explicitly.
