# Marketing direction

Read [foundation](../../references/foundation.md) if evidence is new, then [direction](../../references/direction.md). Do not force an interview when context and delegated judgment are sufficient.

1. Name the audience and buyer separately when they differ. State the job the visitor is trying to finish and what makes it difficult today.
2. Identify one product action that changes that situation. Select the strongest inspectable proof, not the broadest claim.
3. Write the page's primary promise and intended next action. Check that the CTA destination exists or explicitly mark a deliverable placeholder.
4. Map the visitor's questions to sections: what it is, how it works, why believe it, how it fits, what to do next. Omit unsupported comparison/pricing/social-proof blocks.
5. Choose an art direction from the product's brand and interface. Explain the memorable product moment in one sentence. Let content determine density and section count.
6. Record the brief, assumption list, claim IDs, evidence gaps, page list and conversion goal. Continue into composition without a mandatory review pause.

Acceptance: a reader can identify the product, primary benefit and CTA without watching a complete animation; each major promise has a source or a clear qualification. For an existing page, preserve working navigation and product context while fixing the strongest communication defect.
