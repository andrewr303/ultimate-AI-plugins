# Product shot recipe library

Read only the recipe matching the intended proof. These independently authored recipes adapt inspected MIT-source techniques from Agentic Product Demo, Product Launch Motion (current and legacy) and Product Demo Director. Upstream sample films, stills, tracks, customer marks and business metrics are excluded. Video Shotcraft and Brag archives were inaccessible to the transfer tool; no claim is made that their recipes were inspected or integrated.

## R01 — Direct workflow proof

Purpose: show the product doing one useful thing before broad claims. Use an actual recording with entry point, meaningful input/action and complete result. Keep a common case/session through the shot. Select source in/out points from inspected frames, preserving click lead-in and result reading hold. Put a brief hook outside the product unit if needed. Do not accelerate away the only evidence of the action. Verify input precedes result, cursor/action is visible and the result supports the exact promise. Actual footage has stronger causal evidence than a reconstructed end state.

## R02 — Request-to-result continuity

Purpose: preserve the relationship between related UI states. Use a verified replay, same logical surface and deterministic bounds/state changes. Name input, press, process, result arrival and settled frames. Animate the container and content separately; old text exits before new text takes its place. Springs may settle bounds, but important labels should read after arrival. Continuity is useful when it explains the flow; do not force unrelated screens to become one shape. Inspect transition neighborhoods and label replay mode.

## R03 — Complete detail reveal

Purpose: make a dense interface readable without losing meaning. Start with context, then focus a complete native unit such as selected request + status + reply. Keep qualifiers and controls together. Use a connected camera or a deliberate source-bound detail plate at a meaningful boundary. Plan maximum zoom from retained source pixels. Vertical can show a selected case while landscape retains queue context. Reject severed fragments, softened glyphs and a camera that never settles. Check both native pixels and phone-size readability.

## R04 — Measured cursor press

Purpose: make the initiating action unambiguous. Measure target bounds in rendered geometry. Keep cursor/target in the same coordinate system or apply one exact transform. Use arrival, hold spanning press and departure keys; a lone arrival key can let the pointer leave before the click. A modest press/ripple can clarify action. During drag, derive the manipulated value from pointer position. Verify actual output at press time and maximum camera zoom. A decorative cursor clicking the wrong control invalidates the story.

## R05 — Typed input and arriving result

Purpose: explain how a representative input shapes the output. Use the exact verified input string and a playhead-derived prefix. Trigger processing after submission, then reveal a complete result. Keep deterministic sample logic labeled; do not invent network activity. Avoid typing more text than viewers need to understand the request. Preserve error/empty behavior in the source fixture even if the film uses success. Check causal order and settled result reading time.

## R06 — Reviewable output

Purpose: answer a concern about automation or control. Show the suggested result, an actual edit/reorder/inspection action and the product's real review boundary. Keep the final output visible long enough to read. The script can describe the demonstrated bounded control, not blanket reliability or safety. Do not add a human gate the product lacks, or remove one to imply unsupported autonomy. In the Tandem example, editable draft/review is a fixture behavior, not a customer outcome claim.

## R07 — Comparable before/after

Purpose: let viewers inspect a specific supported transformation. Use the same case/data/scale where possible and label before/after clearly. A split can work for a simple difference; sequential states can preserve more readable detail. Avoid decorative wipes that hide the evidence. Do not compare different inputs or claim measured time savings from a sped-up animation. Record both asset IDs, revision and exact claim. Inspect the point at which the difference becomes clear, not only the attractive final frame.

## R08 — Source-backed callout

Purpose: direct attention to a relevant detail without replacing proof with text. Anchor the callout to an inspected source region and preserve its complete semantic unit. Keep callout copy short and linked to the same claim ID. Avoid covering the cursor, status, qualifier or next action. If typesetting a source excerpt, verify every word and identify the explanatory treatment; it is not a native product capture. Review at output size and with captions active.

## R09 — Narration-locked walkthrough

Purpose: align picture to an approved spoken explanation. Preserve the master hash/duration and import actual word/character timing with [timing tools](timing.md). Map each important phrase to an inspected source state/frame. Place cuts between complete thoughts and retain picture coverage and tail. Subtitles match speech; callouts are distinguishable. Do not estimate final timing from word counts or silently speed narration. Measure sync and listen/watch when available, recording unavailable playback honestly.

## R10 — Music-led reveal

Purpose: give a launch film rhythm while the product remains the proof. Use licensed/supplied music or the bundled original local sample score, never excluded source tracks. Inspect its arc and measured onset candidates, then align a few important product events deliberately. Give result states reading space between hits. Time effect peaks to picture events and keep effects restrained. BPM alone is not direction; no requirement says every element must move on every beat. Check final mix peak/loudness and listen when available.

## R11 — Native social recomposition

Purpose: preserve meaning across placements. Start with one campaign hypothesis and re-layout headline, complete product unit, captions and CTA for each ratio. Vertical often isolates a case/result; landscape retains context beside the detail. Square/portrait need their own readable spacing. Use editable safe areas and current official upload specs with source/date. A crop that loses the action or qualifier is a failure even when geometry is valid. Inspect actual encoded files in their target-size players.

## R12 — Seamless homepage loop

Purpose: communicate a short workflow on repeat without a disruptive ending. Choose a bounded action/result and a truthful reset transition. First/last visuals, camera, cursor, velocity and audio boundary must match. Keep product promise available on the surrounding page, provide a poster/alternative and honor reduced motion. Silent muted inline playback is appropriate where selected; do not repurpose a scored ad unchanged. Compare seam frames and watch repeated normal-speed playback; a crossfade may conceal a jump but also blur proof.

## Recipe composition

Combine only the recipes needed for the story. For example, a direct workflow can use measured cursor, detail reveal and native recomposition. The result is still one causal story, not twelve obligatory effects. Record selected recipe IDs in the storyboard to support review and future adaptation. A new brand must supply its own identity, product labels and evidence.
