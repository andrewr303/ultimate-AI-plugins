# Storyboard and composition contract

Record stable shot IDs, integer frame ranges, purpose, source/state, mode, revision, claim IDs, framing, camera, cursor, captions/narration and entry/exit/settled intervals. The output variant points to its storyboard/configuration.

Validate structure and meaning: shots fit duration, action precedes result, narration is covered, captions fit, excerpts exist and zoom preserves usable detail.

Choose a grammar supported by the evidence. A short film does not need every dramatic beat. A feature tour still needs meaningful actions. Make opening, proof and close styleframes before expensive rendering; inspect native output and phone-size viewing. If too dense, simplify the complete unit or recapture responsive UI rather than severing labels.

Use named events such as INPUT_START, PRESS, PROCESS, RESULT_ARRIVE, RESULT_SETTLED, CTA_IN and END. Derive movement and cues from them. Budget cursor arrival, action, result arrival, reading and close. Distinguish useful holds from inert delay.

Camera motion should preserve spatial relation and arrive before reading. A cut may explain better than a morph. Avoid large camera travel while the product itself scrolls unless legibility survives.

Write a composition plan per ratio: safe areas, caption region, product rectangle, title/CTA and maximum crop. Vertical often needs a selected case/result; landscape can retain more context. Square and portrait are deliberate compositions.

Inspect both sides of boundaries and watch low-resolution motion at normal speed. A contact sheet catches clipping/bad states; it cannot establish rhythm or velocity. Recheck actual encoded frames after rendering.
