## 1. US CAN-SPAM (15 USC 7701-7713; 16 CFR Part 316)

### Rule
- **Applies to B2B cold email.** FTC: "The law makes no exception for business-to-business email." It covers any email whose primary purpose is commercial. Confirmed: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **Penalty:** up to $53,088 per violating email, per the FTC page (figure updated Jan 2024). It is inflation-adjusted, so check the current figure. Confirmed (same URL).
- **Required elements, 15 USC 7704(a).** Confirmed: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7704
  - Header information must not be materially false or misleading (a)(1).
  - The subject line must not be likely to mislead about a material fact (a)(2).
  - The message needs a clear and conspicuous identification that it is an advertisement or solicitation (a)(5)(A)(i). This is excused only with prior affirmative consent.
  - It needs a functioning return email or other Internet-based opt-out mechanism, clearly displayed (a)(3). The mechanism must work for at least 30 days after sending.
  - It needs a valid physical postal address of the sender (a)(5)(A)(iii).
- **Opt-out timing, (a)(4)(A).** No covered email may go out more than 10 business days after receiving the request. The address of an opted-out person may not be sold or transferred, except for compliance. Confirmed (7704 URL above).
- **No friction on opt-out, 16 CFR 316.5.** A sender may not require a fee, information beyond the email address and opt-out preferences, or more than a reply email or a single web page visit. Confirmed (summary of the text, via Cornell): https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.5
- **Who is the "sender", 16 CFR 316.2(m).**
  - When one email promotes several advertisers, each qualifying advertiser is deemed a sender. The exception is one that is alone in the From line and meets the listed conditions.
  - The FTC guide says "more than one person may be held responsible", and that a company stays responsible for email sent on its behalf even when it hires another firm. Confirmed: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.2 and the FTC guide above.
- **Harvesting, 7704(b)(1)(A).** Confirmed (7704 URL above).
  - It is an "aggravated violation" to initiate, or to "assist in the origination" of, an email that already violates (a). This includes assisting "through the provision or selection of addresses", if you knew or should fairly have known either of two things.
  - (i) The address was obtained by automated means from a website or online service operated by another person. At the time, that site carried a notice that the operator will not give, sell or transfer its addresses to others for sending or enabling email.
  - (ii) The address was generated by an automated means that combines names, letters or numbers into permutations (a dictionary attack).
  - FTC guide: harvesting and dictionary attacks can bring criminal penalties. Confirmed.
  - 7704(b)(1)(B): the section creates no ownership interest in email addresses.
  - **Reading note.** Scraping a creator's published business email is not itself an aggravated violation. Aggravation needs (1) an email that already breaks (a), and (2) a no-transfer notice on the source site, or guessed addresses. This is my reading of the text, not a holding. **[COUNSEL]** on what "knowledge fairly implied" demands in terms of screening source pages.

### Consequence for a scrape-then-email pipeline
- Inferring `first.last@brand.com`-style addresses is the clearest exposure. Never generate addresses.
- A scraped address is lawful to email only if the message itself meets (a) and the address was not obtained under a no-transfer notice. Record where each address came from.
- Opt-outs must survive re-scrapes. Otherwise a re-ingested address gets emailed again within the 10-business-day window.
- If HypeStake emails creators on behalf of brands, the brand may also be a "sender". Decide whose name is in From and whose postal address is used.

### Guardrail
Pre-send gate. Block the send unless all of these hold:
- The address came from a recorded provenance row and was not generated.
- The address is not on the suppression list.
- The template passes a lint for ad label, postal address, opt-out mechanism and non-deceptive subject and From.

## 2. FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465)

### Rule: Endorsement Guides
The current Guides were revised 26 July 2023 (88 FR 48102). Confirmed (source line): https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-255
- **255.1** (Confirmed, via Cornell).
  - Advertisers are liable for misleading or unsubstantiated statements made through endorsements, "even when the endorser is not liable".
  - Endorsers may be liable for their own statements.
  - Agencies and similar intermediaries "may be liable" for creating or disseminating endorsements they know or should know are deceptive.
  - Advertisers should give guidance to endorsers, monitor compliance and act on problems. The text calls this "not a safe harbor".
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.1
- **255.5** (Confirmed, via Cornell).
  - A material connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously if the audience would not reasonably expect it. This covers business, family or personal relationships, and applies "regardless of whether the advertiser requires an endorsement in return".
  - Examples include a free expensive product (the manufacturer "should advise the influencer and monitor compliance"), employee posts (employers should train and monitor), points programs, affiliate links, and incentivized reviews.
  - Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5
- **FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers"** (Confirmed): https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
  - Free products and perks count as a material connection.
  - Place the disclosure with the endorsement. Do not bury it in a "more" link, an end-of-video note, a profile page or a hashtag cluster.
  - Video needs the disclosure in audio and on screen. Stories and images need it superimposed.
  - Use plain words ("ad", "sponsored", or thanking the brand for a free product). Avoid "sp" or "collab".
  - Platform tools are not necessarily enough on their own. Use them in addition.
  - The page treats the influencer as responsible. It says nothing on brand monitoring.
  - US law can reach foreign posts that affect US consumers.

### Rule: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465)
Effective 21 Oct 2024. Confirmed: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- It prohibits:
  - fake or false testimonials (465.2, including buying or selling them, and brokers);
  - buying positive or negative reviews (465.4);
  - undisclosed insider reviews (465.5);
  - company-controlled "independent" review sites (465.6);
  - review suppression (465.7);
  - knowingly or carelessly buying or distributing fake social-media influence indicators such as bot followers (465.8).
- Hired influencer posts are treated as testimonials under 465.2. A business can be liable if it knew or should have known the influencer misrepresented their use or experience.
- The Rule does not set influencer disclosure requirements. Those stay under the FTC Act and the Guides.
- Civil penalties apply to knowing violations. The FTC page gives no dollar amount. $53,088 per violation appears in law-firm coverage of the Dec 2025 letters (Reported).

### Enforcement 2025-2026
- **No FTC action against an influencer-marketing agency or marketplace found for 2025-2026.**
  - The FTC topic page lists no such case. Confirmed: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/advertisement-endorsements
  - Morgan Lewis (Oct 2026) calls the FTC "relatively quiet on influencer-specific enforcement". Reported: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/10/scrutiny-over-influencer-marketing-and-endorsements-continues-to-heat-up-in-2026
- **Adjacent actions on that topic page** (Confirmed as titles and dates only; I did not read the underlying orders):
  - Sitejabber, final order over misrepresented ratings and reviews, 3 Jan 2025. This is a review-platform intermediary.
  - Fashion Nova, refunds over deceptive review practices, 28 Jan 2025.
  - NextMed, filed 14 Jul 2025 over fake reviews, prices and claims. Final order 3 Dec 2025.
  - Warning letters to 10 companies under the Consumer Review Rule, 22 Dec 2025. Letters are not findings of violation. Detail beyond the title is Reported (law-firm coverage).
- **Publishing.com** ($1.5M, proposed 13 Apr 2026; final order July 2026). Confirmed: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/publishingcom-pay-15-million-misleading-consumers-about-how-much-income-they-could-earn-using
  - Alleged failure to disclose employee or related-party testimonials, and incentivized testimonials (prizes, cash).
  - The order requires disclosure of "unexpected material connections" and of incentives for reviews.
  - This is not an influencer case, but it shows current FTC theory on undisclosed connections.
- The Endorsement Guides give no safe harbor to an intermediary. Intermediary exposure rests on 255.1 text, not on a decided 2025-2026 case.

### Consequence and guardrail
- A platform that brokers deals is inside 255.1 as a disseminator or intermediary. Reduce exposure with contract terms and evidence of guidance and monitoring.
- **Outreach and contract template must require:**
  - disclosure of the material connection with the endorsement itself (placement, wording, audio and on-screen for video);
  - no fabricated experience or claims;
  - no purchased reviews or followers;
  - brand approval of claims, and creator acknowledgment of the disclosure rules.
- **Keep records:** the guidance sent, the live URL of each deliverable, and a disclosure check.
- Do not present follower or engagement metrics to brands if the data is known to be inflated or bot-driven. **[COUNSEL]** on whether Part 465.8 reaches a platform that displays such metrics.

## 3. Privacy: GDPR, UK GDPR, ePrivacy/PECR, CCPA/CPRA, state data-broker laws

### 3a. EU/UK email to named individuals
- **ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC Art. 13** (consent for unsolicited email; soft opt-in; Member States protect legal persons' interests). I could not fetch the text (the EUR-Lex fetch returned nothing and legislation.gov.uk gave a 404), so this is Unverified here. National law varies by Member State. **[COUNSEL]**
- **UK PECR reg. 22:**
  - Marketing email to "individual subscribers" needs prior consent. The soft opt-in applies only where details were obtained in a sale or negotiation of similar products. Confirmed: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22
  - ICO guidance (page blocked, Reported via search snippet): the email rule does not apply to corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships, some public bodies). Sole traders and non-LLP partnerships are individual subscribers and are treated as individuals. The ICO suggests keeping a "do not email" list of objecting corporate subscribers. Source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
  - **Many creators are sole traders or natural persons.** The corporate-subscriber exemption is therefore unlikely to cover them, and a personal-looking address at any domain is personal data. Treat EU/UK creators as individuals unless the entity is verified as a company.
- **UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025:** reportedly raises the PECR maximum fine to GDPR-level (£17.5M or 4% of turnover). I could not confirm the commencement date. Reported: https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-pecr-reform-rules-relating-to-electronic-marketing-and-cookies-in-the-uk
- **GDPR Art. 14** (data not obtained from the person). Confirmed (unofficial mirror of the text): https://gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/
  - Provide controller identity, purposes, legal basis, categories, recipients, source, retention, legitimate interests and rights.
  - Timing: within a reasonable period and at most one month, or at the first communication if the data is used to contact the person.
  - 14(5)(b) exempts cases where notice is impossible or a disproportionate effort. Regulators read this narrowly.
- **Bisnode (Poland).** The Polish DPA fined Bisnode (about PLN 943,000) for not giving Art. 14 notice to people whose data came from public business registers, and the Supreme Administrative Court dismissed its appeal on 19 Sep 2023.
  - The disproportionate-effort argument was rejected: transparency is the rule and exceptions are read narrowly.
  - The case is useful because "the data was public" did not remove the notice duty.
  - Confirmed: https://uodo.gov.pl/en/553/1572
- **Legitimate interest.** EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 set a three-step test (interest, necessity, balancing). I could not confirm whether the final version is adopted. Reported: https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/innovation-law-insights/2024/innovation-law-insights-18-october-2024
- **CNIL, B2B prospecting.** Confirmed: https://www.cnil.fr/fr/communication-electronique-quelles-regles (dated 10 Jun 2026)
  - Prior consent is not systematically required if the message relates to the person's professional activity.
  - The person must be told the source of the data and the purpose.
  - There must be a simple way to object.
  - CNIL recommends a suppression ("liste repoussoir").
- **CNIL, guessed professional addresses.** Confirmed (fiche dated 16 Oct 2026): https://cnil.fr/fr/node/169044
  - Reconstructing an address can rest on legitimate interest only if conditions are met. It is "ni automatique ni systématique".
  - The practice must not disproportionately affect people. Where a person has no identifiable online presence, or has protected their contact details, they cannot reasonably expect reconstruction.
  - Any objection must immediately end reconstruction processing for that person.
  - Keep reconstructed addresses only if a first effective contact happens within a reasonable time.
  - The information duty still applies.
  - This reinforces the "never guess addresses" guardrail on EU grounds as well as CAN-SPAM grounds.

**Consequence:** For EU/UK creators, scraping a published business contact is only the start. You still need a documented legitimate-interest assessment, an Art. 14 notice in the first message, an objection route, a suppression list, and probably country gating. UK/EU PECR or national rules may demand consent for sole traders. **[COUNSEL]**

### 3b. US state privacy and data-broker law
- **CCPA/CPRA: "publicly available" excluded from personal information.** Confirmed (statute text via california.public.law): https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1798.140
  - It covers government records, information a business "has a reasonable basis to believe is lawfully made available to the general public", and information the consumer made available without restricting its audience.
  - **Applicability:** the CCPA applies only to a "business" meeting one of three tests: over $25M revenue (inflation-adjusted), buying, selling or sharing the data of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing. An early-stage startup may be below all three. Confirmed (same URL).
- **B2B exemption expired 1 Jan 2023.** Business-contact data is now covered like other personal information, where the CCPA applies. Reported: https://www.troutman.com/insights/californias-looming-privacy-deadline-for-personnel-and-b2b-data/
- **Notice at collection, Civ. Code 1798.100(a)-(b).** A business that controls collection must give notice at or before collection. A third party that controls collection can satisfy this by a conspicuous homepage notice. Data minimization applies under 1798.100(c). Confirmed: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1798.100
- **California Delete Act / DROP.** Confirmed: https://privacy.ca.gov/drop-for-data-brokers/
  - A "data broker" knowingly collects **and sells** to third parties the personal information of consumers with whom it has no direct relationship.
  - Brokers must register annually (fee $6,000) and start processing DROP deletion requests on 1 Aug 2026, at least every 45 days. Penalty $200 per day (failure to register) and $200 per day per deletion request (failure to delete).
  - The page also says a 30-day cadence applies from 1 Jan 2027 (AB 883). Its own figures conflict, so verify.
  - Collecting for your own outreach is not selling. Selling or sharing a creator database or enrichment feed with brands could make HypeStake a broker. **[COUNSEL]**
- **Texas (SB 2121, effective 1 Sep 2025).** The data-broker definition was reportedly broadened to a business that "collects, processes, or transfers personal data" it did not collect directly from the individual. Thresholds, per the same coverage: over 50% of revenue from such data, or any revenue from processing the data of over 50,000 individuals. Civil penalty $100 per day, reportedly capped at $10,000 per 12 months. Reported: https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/07/texas-legislature-amends-data-broker-law-to-broaden-definition-arguably-narrow-applicability-thresholds/ and https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250904-texas-expands-and-modifies-data-broker-registration-law. **[COUNSEL]** on whether "directly" and the revenue tests catch a scrape-and-store pipeline.
- **Vermont (H.211, Act 138, signed 16 Jun 2026; effective 1 Jan 2027).** Reportedly expands data-broker obligations, adds consumer rights, and raises fees and penalties. I did not confirm the fee or penalty figures against the enacted text. Reported: https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/vermont-enacts-significant-amendments-to-data-broker-legislation
- **Other states:** not researched (Oregon, and others with data-broker registration). Unverified.
- **Outside the US:** Canada (CASL) and other national spam laws were not researched. Unverified. Gate by country.

## 4. Scraping legality and platform terms

### Case law (what it does and does not say)
- **Van Buren v. United States (2021).** CFAA "exceeds authorized access" and "without authorization" turn on a "gates-up-or-down" question. Footnote 8 left open whether only code-based limits count, or also contract or policy limits. Confirmed: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-783
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn (9th Cir., Apr 2022).**
  - On remand after Van Buren, the court again affirmed a preliminary injunction. Scraping publicly available profiles likely is not "without authorization" under the CFAA when no authentication gate exists.
  - It did not decide contract or trespass claims. Reported (Justia fetch blocked; summaries via search): https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-16783/17-16783-2022-04-18.html
  - **Aftermath.** In Nov 2022 the district court held LinkedIn's user-agreement bans on scraping and fake profiles enforceable (contract). A Dec 2022 consent judgment then imposed $500,000, a permanent injunction, and destruction of scraped data and derived algorithms. It cited fake accounts and password-protected pages. Reported: https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/
  - Lesson: the CFAA was not what made hiQ lose. Contract and common-law claims did.
- **Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge Chen, 23 Jan 2024).**
  - Summary judgment for Bright Data on the contract claim. Meta's terms were read to bind logged-in users, not logged-out scraping, and not after account termination. Meta later dropped the case.
  - Reported: https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/
  - Secondary sources disagree on the survival-clause reasoning. Read the opinion.
  - It is a district-court ruling only. Meta's terms have since been rewritten (below).
- **X Corp v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge Alsup).** Reported (Goldman blog): https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/01/catching-up-on-the-heavyweight-scraping-battle-between-x-and-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm
  - May 2024: X's complaint was dismissed.
  - 26 Nov 2024: leave to amend was largely granted. Contract claims about scraped data were held preempted by the Copyright Act. Claims about access, based on server impairment, survived. Trespass to chattels, tortious interference and a UCL "fraudulent" claim proceeded.
  - Bright Data filed antitrust counterclaims on 17 Dec 2024.
  - I found no final judgment and could not confirm the current docket status after early 2025.
- **Reddit v. Anthropic.** Filed June 2025 in San Francisco Superior Court (contract, trespass, unjust enrichment, tortious interference, UCL). The federal court remanded it to state court in late March 2026, holding the state claims not preempted. That ruling was on jurisdiction, not the merits. Reported: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/northern-district-of-california-court-holds-state-tort-and-contract-claims-not-preempted-by-federal-copyright-act-remands-reddit-v-anthropic-to-state-court
- **Reddit v. SerpApi, Oxylabs, AWMProxy and Perplexity (S.D.N.Y., filed 22 Oct 2025).**
  - Theory: DMCA 1201 circumvention of Google's SearchGuard to scrape Google results containing Reddit content.
  - Reddit sued the **downstream buyer (Perplexity)** together with the scraping vendors.
  - Docket status is inconsistent across sources. One reports a motion to dismiss denied in part on 31 Jul 2026. Reported: https://ppc.land/serpapi-pushes-to-kill-reddits-dmca-suit-over-google-scraping/
- I found no 2026 appellate ruling that changes the logged-out public-data CFAA position (hiQ, 2022). Reported: https://www.joinmassive.com/blog/myth-scraping-is-hacking (vendor blog).

### What scraper vendors do and do not shift
- **A vendor cannot assume statutory duties that attach to you.**
  - The FTC guide says a company remains responsible for email sent on its behalf. Confirmed (Section 1 URL).
  - CAN-SPAM 7704(b) reaches anyone who "assist[s] in the origination" through "provision or selection of addresses". Confirmed.
  - GDPR Art. 14 attaches to the controller who holds the data. Confirmed.
- **Downstream buyers get sued too.** Reddit named Perplexity alongside its scraping vendors. Reported (S.D.N.Y. coverage above).
- **Meta's terms demand indemnity from collectors and bar selling the data.** Confirmed (ADC Terms).
- Vendor MSAs commonly disclaim responsibility for the buyer's use and for target-site terms. I did not read any vendor's terms, so this is Unverified. Get the actual vendor contract, a provenance statement, and any indemnity reviewed. **[COUNSEL]**
- **Official-API route.** The YouTube API route forbids scraping and non-API access. Whether the API exposes channel business emails is Unverified. Do not assume it does.

## 5. robots.txt and polite fetching (RFC 9309)

**What RFC 9309 says** (Confirmed): https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html
- Rules are "not a form of access authorization." They are not a permission grant, a legal defense, or a substitute for security.
- 4xx on robots.txt: the crawler may access any resource. 5xx or unreachable: assume complete disallow.
- Do not rely on a cached robots.txt for more than 24 hours unless it is unreachable.
- The most specific (longest) matching rule wins. On a tie, allow wins. No match means allowed.
- User-agent matching is case-insensitive. Fall back to the `*` group, and if there is no `*` group, no rules apply.
- Follow at least five redirects. Parse at least 500 KiB.

**Why it matters beyond etiquette.**
- Meta's ADC Terms require honoring robots.txt (Confirmed).
- YouTube's terms exempt only search engines that follow its robots.txt (Confirmed).
- Whether ignoring robots.txt creates legal exposure is not settled in anything I read. **[COUNSEL]**

**Proposed policy for a polite fetcher.** These are my recommendations, not requirements from the RFC.
1. Fetch and cache robots.txt per origin, with a 24-hour maximum age. Apply the RFC status handling above. Treat 5xx as disallow.
2. Honor `Disallow` for the product's own named user-agent token, then `*`. Also honor `noindex` and `nofollow` robots meta signals for discovery.
3. Use an identifiable user-agent string with a contact URL or email. Never spoof a browser or another crawler.
4. Throttle per host (suggested default 1 request per second or slower, concurrency 1). Honor `Crawl-delay` as a conservative hint (non-standard, not in RFC 9309). Back off exponentially on 429 or 503 and honor `Retry-After`.
5. Fetch only what you need: one contact or about page per creator, not a site crawl.
6. Do not log in, create accounts, solve CAPTCHAs, rotate IPs to evade blocks, or retry after an explicit block. A block means stop for that host.
7. Stop on a received complaint or cease-and-desist and route it to counsel.
8. Record robots decision, status code and timestamp per fetch in the provenance log.

## 6. Prompt injection from scraped pages

A skill that has an agent read scraped creator pages is exposed to **indirect prompt injection**. OWASP LLM01:2025 defines it as external content, such as web pages or files, containing instructions that alter the model's behavior, possibly invisible to humans. It lists impacts including unauthorized use of connected tools and manipulation of decisions. Its mitigations are: constrain model behavior, validate output formats with deterministic code, filter inputs and outputs, enforce least privilege with tool calls handled in application code, require human approval for high-risk actions, segregate and label untrusted content, and run adversarial testing. It says it is unclear whether foolproof prevention exists. Confirmed: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/. The UK NCSC has argued (Dec 2025) that prompt injection may never be fully mitigated and that systems should limit the damage a compromised output can do, treating the model as a "confusable deputy". ## Compliance guardrails to encode (testable)

1. **Provenance required.** Every contact record carries `source_url`, `retrieved_at`, `collection_method`, `robots_decision`, and `published_for_business_contact` (bool). Test: a send call on a record missing any field is rejected.
2. **No address generation.** No code path builds or guesses an email from name plus domain patterns. Test: a lint or grep check finds no pattern-generation helper, and a test confirms the importer rejects an address with no source URL.
3. **No-transfer notice screen.** If the source page or its terms state that addresses will not be given, sold or transferred for email, the address is blocked. Test: a fixture page containing such a notice yields `blocked`.
4. **Template lint before send.** Each outgoing email has a non-deceptive subject, an accurate From and Reply-To, an advertisement or solicitation label, a postal address, and an opt-out mechanism. Test: removing any element fails the lint.
5. **Opt-out with no friction.** Reply or one link, with no login, fee or extra data (316.5). Test: opt-out endpoint returns success with only the email address in the request.
6. **Suppression is durable.** Normalize and hash emails, and key on the domain as well. Suppress immediately and never later than 10 business days. The mechanism works at least 30 days after each send. Test: re-ingest an opted-out address and confirm the send is blocked, and confirm opt-out address is never exported or transferred.
7. **Country gating, default deny.** Unknown or EU/EEA/UK country means no cold email until counsel approves a policy. Sole traders and natural persons count as individuals. Test: a record with `country=null` or `country=DE` is not sendable.
8. **Art. 14 notice in first contact.** First email to any EU/UK person includes controller identity, source, purpose, legal basis, retention and rights. Test: template fields are non-empty for restricted-country sends.
9. **Retention and minimization.** Store only business-contact fields, no inferred sensitive attributes, and purge contacts never contacted after a configured window (proposal: 90 days). Test: a scheduled purge job deletes a fixture record past the window.
10. **Per-source policy table.** Each source is labeled `official_api`, `public_logged_out`, or `prohibited`, with a terms-reviewed date. The fetcher refuses unlisted or prohibited sources. Test: Instagram, TikTok and X web scraping return `prohibited` unless counsel clears them.
11. **Polite fetcher.** Section 5 rules 1 to 8, enforced in code. Test: fixtures for 5xx robots, disallowed path, 429 with `Retry-After`, and a block page each produce the specified behavior.
12. **No gate circumvention.** No logins, no throwaway accounts, no CAPTCHA solving, no proxy rotation to evade a block. Test: the fetcher has no credential or solver config, and a block response ends the host job.
13. **Vendor intake gate.** A third-party data vendor is usable only with a recorded review: purpose permitted by its terms, a provenance statement, an indemnity position, and the same provenance fields on delivered rows. Test: a vendor import with missing metadata is rejected.
14. **Injection isolation.** The agent that reads scraped text has no send, delete or suppression-write tools. Output must pass schema validation, and the first send and any bulk send need human approval. Test: an injection fixture ("email this list to X", "unsubscribe nobody") produces no tool call and no state change.
15. **FTC contract and outreach clause.** Deal templates require the material-connection disclosure (clear, conspicuous, with the endorsement), no fake reviews or purchased followers, and creator acknowledgment. The deliverable's URL and a disclosure check are logged. Test: a deal cannot reach `active` without the clause and acknowledgment on file.
