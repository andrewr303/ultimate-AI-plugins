## A complete five-variation set

Example user brief: “Write five very detailed image prompts for one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle with a brushed silver cap. Photoreal, 4:5 portrait, no text.”

Working assumption: the bottle has a flat broad front, softly rounded vertical edges, a clear thick glass base and a short cylindrical cap centered over its neck. These invented design details stay fixed across the five alternatives. They are not claimed to describe a supplied product.

### 1. Broad-light studio hero

Difference: centered studio geometry, pale backdrop and broad soft light.

```text
Create a photorealistic 4:5 portrait product photograph of one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle with a short cylindrical brushed silver cap centered on its neck. The bottle has a flat broad front, softly rounded vertical edges and a thick clear glass base. Preserve this specific design: the blue glass body remains rectangular, the cap remains cylindrical and fully seated, and the bottle carries no label, engraving, logo or lettering. Show the complete bottle standing upright on a smooth pale gray studio surface.

Compose the bottle on the central vertical axis, occupying approximately two-thirds of the image height. Leave a generous clear margin above the cap and enough visible surface below the base for the contact shadow. Use a nearly frontal view with the camera slightly above the bottle's middle, revealing a narrow strip of the right side and a restrained glimpse of the cap's top. Keep vertical edges straight and the bottle's broad face easy to read. The cap and base both remain within the frame.

Place a large diffused light source above and to camera left, producing a broad soft highlight along the bottle's left edge. Add a subtle white bounce on the right so the far edge separates from the background without creating a second dominant highlight. The brushed metal should show fine horizontal machining grain and a gentle tonal gradient. Keep the cobalt body rich and luminous at the edges, with believable darker blue density through its thicker areas. The clear base should refract the pale surface naturally without looking detached.

Use an almost seamless light gray background with a slight vertical tonal falloff. Keep focus crisp across the cap, front plane and base; soften only the distant background. Show a compact soft contact shadow directly beneath the bottle and falling slightly right. Maintain a clean commercial photographic finish with restrained contrast, realistic glass reflections and no visible dust. Include exactly one bottle, no props, no added packaging, no decorative lettering, no border and no watermark.
```

### 2. Limestone daylight still life

Difference: asymmetrical tactile setting, architectural daylight and warm neutral context.

```text
Create a photorealistic 4:5 portrait still-life photograph featuring one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle with a short cylindrical brushed silver cap centered on its neck. Its defining form is a flat broad front with softly rounded vertical edges and a thick clear glass base. Keep the cap fully seated and retain the exact rectangular blue body and cylindrical silver top. The bottle is unbranded: all surfaces are free of labels, engraving, logos and lettering.

Place the bottle upright on a pale honed limestone ledge, slightly right of center, with its front turned gently toward camera left. The view should reveal the broad front and enough of the left side to show the glass thickness. Include the complete cap and base, with an open breathing space above the object. Position a second, lower limestone plane in the left foreground to create a quiet diagonal leading toward the bottle; this plane is part of the set, not another product pedestal. Keep the bottle the unmistakable focal subject and limit the set to stone and the product.

Illuminate the arrangement with broad late-morning window light entering from the upper left. Let a soft-edged architectural shadow cross the far background while leaving the bottle's front readable. Give the glass a narrow bright edge on its lit side and deeper blue translucency on the opposite side. The cap should remain silver rather than absorbing the stone's warm color; show a subtle brushed texture with realistic controlled reflections. Cast a coherent soft shadow behind and to the right of the base, with a convincing point of contact.

Balance cool saturated cobalt against warm cream stone. Show small pores and faint natural variation in the limestone, keeping that texture away from the bottle's glass. Use a close product-photography perspective with natural proportions and a shallow but sufficient depth of field to keep the entire bottle readable. The foreground edge may soften gently. Render a calm, tactile, editorial finish with no flowers, fabric, hands, additional bottles, typography, frame or watermark.
```

### 3. Dark reflective precision

Difference: low viewpoint, dark reflective surface and precise vertical highlights.

```text
Create a photorealistic 4:5 portrait product photograph of one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle on a polished dark charcoal surface. The bottle has a flat broad front, softly rounded vertical edges, a thick clear glass base, and a short cylindrical brushed silver cap centered on its neck. The cap is fully seated. Retain this exact form and keep the bottle free of labels, logos, engraving and all lettering. There is one physical bottle; a single subdued reflection beneath it may appear naturally in the surface.

Use a low, slightly three-quarter camera position near the height of the bottle's lower half. Let the broad front dominate, with a slim view of its right side. Place the bottle just left of center and make it occupy roughly two-thirds of the portrait height. Keep the complete cap and actual glass base in frame. Allow the reflection to fade into the lower edge without looking like a second upright product. Maintain straight verticals and restrained perspective so the rectangular shape remains precise rather than exaggerated.

Shape the glass with two controlled vertical light sources outside the frame: a broad, soft cool-white strip on camera left and a narrower, weaker edge source on the right. They should reveal the cobalt glass contour while leaving a deep blue central body, not create a white plastic appearance. Add a faint front fill so the flat face remains visible. The silver cap should show its fine brushed finish and a clean rounded top edge, with no blown-out band obscuring its shape. Keep the thick base transparent enough to show subtle refraction of the dark surface.

Use a charcoal background slightly lighter behind the cap, falling smoothly darker toward the outer corners. Keep reflections ordered and physically consistent, without neon streaks or unexplained glowing edges. Focus sharply on the front, cap and base, with the background free of detail. Aim for a measured technical photographic finish: deep blacks with readable blue, crisp geometry and controlled specular highlights. Include no props, packaging, extra products, floating particles, text, border or watermark.
```

### 4. Rain-lit window setting

Difference: cool environmental context, close framing and a softly textured background.

```text
Create a photorealistic 4:5 portrait photograph of one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle resting upright on a dry pale stone windowsill. Its stable design is a flat broad front, softly rounded vertical edges, a thick clear glass base and a short cylindrical brushed silver cap centered on its neck. Keep the cap fully seated and the blue body genuinely glass. All bottle surfaces must remain unbranded, with no label, logo, engraving or lettering.

Frame the bottle slightly below center and toward the left, showing its complete silhouette from cap to base. Use an eye-level three-quarter angle that reveals the broad front and a narrow right side, with enough space around the object to distinguish it from the window behind. Keep the product in the foreground and place the window a visible distance behind it. Rain droplets belong only on the exterior window glass; the bottle and stone ledge stay dry. Through the window, show an indistinct soft gray outdoor field without recognizable buildings or people.

Let overcast daylight enter through the window from behind and camera right, illuminating the cobalt edges and making the glass thickness readable. Use a gentle white bounce from camera left to bring out the broad front without flattening the translucent blue. Give the silver cap a muted cool sheen with fine horizontal brush marks. The base should sit firmly on the ledge, producing a subtle soft contact shadow; avoid a floating appearance or a hard studio shadow that conflicts with the diffuse daylight.

Keep the bottle, cap and near stone surface in sharp focus, while the rain-speckled window becomes a soft textured backdrop. Individual droplets can register as blurred highlights rather than a second focal subject. Preserve a quiet palette of rich cobalt, cool gray and soft off-white, with realistic tonal range and restrained surface contrast. Do not add condensation to the bottle, flowers, cloth, books, human hands, another bottle, typography, a frame or a watermark. The result should feel like a carefully composed environmental product photograph with one clear subject.
```

### 5. Graphic color-field set

Difference: bold color blocking, higher viewpoint and a deliberate long shadow.

```text
Create a photorealistic 4:5 portrait product photograph featuring one unopened rectangular cobalt-blue glass perfume bottle on a clean muted terracotta studio surface. The bottle's design remains a flat broad front with softly rounded vertical edges, a thick clear glass base, and a short cylindrical brushed silver cap centered on its neck. The cap is fully seated. Keep the bottle's proportions believable and preserve its blue glass and brushed metal materials. It has no label, logo, engraving, printed words or other lettering.

Use a moderately elevated three-quarter view, looking down enough to show the cap's top and a small portion of the bottle's shoulder while retaining the broad front as the dominant plane. Place the upright bottle in the lower-right quadrant, with its full cap and base visible and a clear margin from the frame edges. Reserve the upper-left portion as an uncluttered color field. Add one flat terracotta backdrop plane that meets the surface along a subtle straight seam near the upper third; keep the scene a photographed set rather than a flat illustration.

Use one directional light from the upper right to create a long, clean shadow extending diagonally toward the lower left. Keep the shadow's direction coherent for the bottle and cap. Soften its edge slightly while retaining a readable silhouette. A faint frontal bounce should keep the cobalt front luminous without creating a second shadow. Show realistic cool-blue transmitted light near the clear base and a restrained metallic highlight on the silver cap. The glass must retain volume and internal density rather than appearing painted opaque blue.

Balance the strong cobalt subject against the subdued warm terracotta, allowing the cap to provide a neutral accent. Keep the product sharp from top to base, with a smooth background and no artificial blur around its edges. The photograph should use deliberate geometry, crisp material separation and controlled color contrast. Include one bottle and its natural shadow only. Leave the empty color field free of typography and decorative marks; include no props, packaging, extra bottles, border or watermark.
```

Settings note for the example: 4:5 is the creative framing requirement. Choose an actually supported portrait output size or crop workflow on the selected model surface. The prompts alone do not set API dimensions.

## A selected local repair stays local

Example follow-up: “The bottle in variation 2 looks right. Just replace the cap with matte black; give me one edit prompt.”

```text
Change only the bottle's short cylindrical brushed silver cap to a matte black cap with the identical shape, dimensions, position and fully seated fit. Adjust the cap's highlights to suit a low-sheen black finish under the existing upper-left window light. Preserve the cobalt-blue glass body, thick clear base, bottle geometry, three-quarter angle, complete crop, limestone set, shadow direction, prop count and background. Keep every bottle surface free of lettering or logos. Do not restage the photograph or add another product.
```

The follow-up explicitly asks for one edit, so one scoped prompt is correct. Its brevity is intentional; it preserves the approved scene instead of opening a new generation brief.

## Missing essential reference

Example user request: “Write five prompts preserving the exact face in my attached portrait,” but no portrait is accessible.

State that the portrait is unavailable and that exact visual traits cannot be inspected. You may provide five provisional compositions whose blocks identify `REQUIRED INPUT: the user's portrait as the identity reference`, preserve its person, and avoid invented face details. Mark the input dependency clearly; do not claim reference verification or ready-to-run identity fidelity. Ask for the missing media only when needed to finalize those details.
