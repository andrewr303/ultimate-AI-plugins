# Detailed direction without leaving accidental gaps

Use concrete decisions that affect the visible result. The goal is controlled direction, not more adjectives.

## Subject and space

Describe what a viewer can recognize: age presentation only when relevant and supplied or intentionally fictional, face/hair silhouette, wardrobe cut/material/color, footwear, accessories, posture and performance. For products, fix silhouette, scale, finish, seams, label location, opening mechanism and identifying marks. Never infer identity or sensitive attributes from a reference. Use a fictional visual specification when generating an original character.

Define the set as a usable space: subject location, camera side, foreground/midground/background, horizon, doors/windows and movement routes. Place props relative to subjects. “She picks it up” needs an identified object, a starting position, a hand and an end position. If the shot involves contact, show approach, contact, response and settling. Do not make a solid object pass through a hand, wall or tabletop.

## Camera and focus

Choose the composition first, then camera language that supports it:

- Establish scale with a wide frame, reveal information with a measured move, isolate an action with a close detail, or keep intimacy through an eye-level medium frame.
- State height and angle; keep screen direction legible. An orbit changes the camera's side—plan its relation to the action and the next cut.
- Describe a path with an endpoint: push from medium to tight, track parallel at walking pace, rise just enough to reveal a skyline. “Dynamic cinematic camera” leaves the important choice unspecified.
- Set a clear focus subject and whether focus remains fixed, tracks a subject or transfers between two named planes. Do not request deep focus and extremely shallow focus for the same moment.
- Specify stabilized, handheld or fixed support only when it matters. A handheld feel can still be restrained. Avoid unmotivated lens jargon or impossible numerical combinations.

## Temporal choreography

Use one dominant action per short shot. A multi-action shot needs time for preparation and payoff. A six-second scene rarely accommodates several location changes, a long speech, a wardrobe change and a complicated interaction.

Give each beat a purpose: establish, initiate, develop, reveal, settle. Timestamps are editorial targets, not guaranteed frame boundaries. Do not over-specify many tiny intervals the model cannot reliably follow. If a user gives exact total duration, add intervals and verify the total. Distinguish an intentional dissolve overlap from an accidental timeline overlap; explicitly state the final assembled runtime if overlaps are used.

Keep environment movement subordinate: breeze direction, steam drift, moving water, people in the distance. Match wind in hair, cloth and plants. Reflections should track their objects and light. Keep gravity and inertia consistent unless the user requested a fantasy transformation.

## Light, color and surface

Choose a motivated source, direction, softness, contrast and practicals. State which side of the subject catches the light and how the shadow reads. Preserve the key-light direction across matching shots. A move past a window can motivate a light change; an unexplained change of sun direction creates drift.

Use a restrained palette with distinct subject/background colors. Materials should respond plausibly: brushed metal broadens highlights along its grain; matte paper has a soft diffuse response; wet stone carries directional reflections; thin fabric changes shape in air. Name detail once where it belongs rather than applying “textured” to every surface.

Specify medium deliberately: live-action realism, stop motion, ink animation, stylized 3D, cut-paper, archival documentary or other requested form. If changing medium across variations, retain the user's factual requirements and state the change. Never silently reinterpret a request for realism as animation.

## Performance, dialogue and audio

Direct a readable intention and physical expression: a delayed glance, a measured breath, shoulders releasing, a hand pausing before contact. Avoid prescribing several incompatible emotions at once. Assign the speaker before quoting a line. Keep language, spelling and punctuation intact. Put speech within a plausible interval; reduce or split it only with an explicit explanation if the user's duration makes it impossible.

Separate layers: room tone/location ambience, practical event sound, speech, music. Describe onsets and fades around actions and cuts. State no dialogue, no music or deliberate silence only when it serves the chosen brief. Do not accidentally ask for silence and also a rich audible ambience. “No speech; quiet natural ambience only” is different from a silent export.

Native audio support, audio upload support and voice controls are distinct capabilities. A written voice description is creative direction, not a cloned voice or attached reference. For a surface without the requested sound feature, provide a separate editorial sound cue plan.

## Continuity and transitions

Track identity, costume, prop count, prop ownership, hand, action phase, subject direction, eyeline, camera side, weather, time, light and sound. A match cut needs matching motion or composition; a hard cut can change location but must preserve the intended story. An intentional montage can vary place and time when those changes are explicitly directed.

For generated clips to be assembled later, end with usable settling or action overlap where appropriate. Repeating a short stable state in two generation units can help an editor choose a cut, but no model-independent “seamless” guarantee follows. Keep final delivery timing explicit when handles are requested.

## Minimal uncertainty review

Ask internally: What must be visible? What must happen? In what order? Where does the camera begin and end? What changes? What cannot change? What does the viewer hear? What exact state must the next shot inherit? Fill the answers that matter. Leave decorative details open only when the user permits that freedom or extra specification would create conflict.
